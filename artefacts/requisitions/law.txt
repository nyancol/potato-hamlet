POTATO HAMLET — PURCHASE REQUISITIONS

1. Any citizen of the hamlet may lodge a purchase requisition with the
   townhall. The town's standing schedule fixes the goods and the sum, so a
   requisition is always for the standing item at the standing price.

2. A citizen may hold no more than one open requisition at a time. Lodging a
   second while the first is open shall be refused.

3. A requisition may be withdrawn by the citizen who lodged it at any time
   before it is decided, including after the fourteenth day has passed. A
   withdrawal therefore commonly ends a requisition part-way through its
   consideration window rather than at the deadline. Withdrawal closes the
   requisition; it does not erase it, and it stays in the record as
   withdrawn.

4. The Requisition Officer shall decide every open requisition on or before
   the fourteenth day. The Officer shall either
   approve the requisition or refuse it, and shall record which.

5. An approval authorises the purchase at the sum asked. A refusal does not.

6. A requisition decided after the fourteenth day is late, and the delay is
   recorded against the Officer.

7. The Requisition Officer is the oldest citizen of the hamlet who is not
   themselves a requisitioner. If every citizen holds an open requisition the
   post is vacant, and the duty waits; no other citizen may take it.

8. Requisitions stand independently of one another. One being decided, refused
   or withdrawn has no bearing on any other, and any number of them may be
   open at the same time.
